# The First Session and the Next Three Hours

Version 0.5 preserves the short narrative prologue, then opens into a deliberately long idle-game grind. The first session should explain why the player is working; later sessions should make mastery, accumulation, and visible restoration satisfying over days rather than minutes.

## Prologue pacing guardrails

| Milestone | Target | Deterministic simulation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Understand the premise | under 30 seconds | player-paced comic |
| Inspect the manuscript | under 45 seconds | player-paced guide |
| First Knowledge reward | 6 seconds after inspection | 0.1 minutes |
| Recover the first word | under 2 minutes | 0.1 minutes |
| Understand a complete sentence | under 7 minutes | 3.6 minutes |
| Reveal Al-Jahiz | under 10 minutes | 6.1 minutes |
| Gather the desk materials | under 13 minutes | 7.4 minutes |
| Restore the Keeper’s Desk | under 15 minutes | 8.8 minutes |

The deterministic simulation advances in one-second increments, chooses the newest useful Language activity, gathers exactly five timber and four stone, and uses the same game functions as the interface. It is a balance guardrail, not a promise about every human player.

## First-session flow

1. View a narrated four-panel comic establishing Baghdad’s permanent Darkness and the quest to restore light.
2. Enter the ruined House with the torn manuscript and worn hammer.
3. Follow the researcher’s speech bubble to inspect the manuscript while the ghost remains unknown.
4. Trace letters, recover roots, grammar, and eloquence, then understand Al-Jahiz’s first complete joke.
5. Salvage five timber and four stone from the room.
6. Gather 30 Knowledge and repair the Keeper’s Desk.
7. See a distant lamp answer and the Darkness meter fall from 100% to 99%.
8. Name Ignorance and open the larger task: rebuild an institution that can circulate knowledge.

## The first long project

The Keeper’s Desk is the handoff from tutorial to idle progression:

1. The Scholar’s Ledger lists persistent Story, House, Civic, and Daily tasks. These are goals, not a scheduler.
2. Current Work runs one selected activity repeatedly while the player is present or away, for up to 24 hours.
3. The player develops six visible disciplines: Language, Scribing, Gathering, Translation, Mathematics, and Architecture.
4. Each repeated activity gains its own Mastery, becoming faster and eventually more productive.
5. Restoring the Scriptorium requires 6,000 Knowledge, 800 timber, and 600 stone. It is intended to take sustained play and several returns.
6. The player can set a target of 10 or 100 repetitions, let an activity continue indefinitely, or stop it at any time.
7. Restoration lowers Campaign Darkness from 99% to 96% and begins the eastern-school civic project.

The grind is not filler: Language and Scribing transform an unreadable manuscript into something that can serve the city, while Gathering and later Architecture rebuild the place that makes this circulation possible.

## The eastern-school campaign

The first civic campaign proves institutional scale rather than a single charitable errand:

1. Decipher the damaged primer once.
2. Prepare ink and produce 20 readable primer copies while preserving the original.
3. Deliver 20 copies to the eastern school.
4. Relight the school and lower permanent Campaign Darkness from 96% to 95%.
5. Unlock Translation as the next major discipline and story frontier.

After the school is relit, three small Daily Duties appear once per local day: Learn, Make, and Serve. They create three temporary points of encroachment. Completing the duties removes those points for that day. Missed duties never stack, destroy progress, or break a streak.

## Economy notes

- Knowledge is both a resource and proof of study; applied work consumes some of it.
- Timber and stone support the first major restoration grind.
- Ink, folios, and primer copies establish stackable crafted goods.
- Discipline levels unlock later fields; activity Mastery rewards staying with specific work.
- The repaired desk grants +10% Knowledge to Language work.
- A 24-hour away-time cap makes daily returns useful without demanding constant checking.
- Markets, manuscript trade, additional rooms, and multiple civic campaigns come after this foundation is shown to be enjoyable.

## Validation gate

Before expanding the economy, playtests should establish that players understand the separation between the Ledger and Current Work, enjoy choosing a long activity, notice Mastery gains, feel that the Scriptorium target is ambitious but legible, and connect every grind to the House’s recovery from Ignorance.
